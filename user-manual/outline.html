Outline

See the whole script as structure: acts, sequences, sections and scenes, in one list you can reorder.

Opening the Outline

Choose View > Panels > Outline. The Outline takes the place of the script page while it is open, and the menu item shows a tick. To go back to the script, click the × in the Outline’s header, choose the menu item again, or click a scene, which also jumps to that scene in the script.

The Outline shares the editor area with the Beat Board, Statistics and the Story Timeline, so opening one of them closes the others. Opening Table Read also closes the Outline so you can follow the reading in the script. The Outline always starts closed when you reopen the app.

What It Shows

  • Acts — every New Act element in the script starts an act, shown by its name (or ACT 1, ACT 2…). Scenes before the first act break are grouped under PROLOGUE.
  • Sequences — a run of scenes with the same sequence label gets a header row with the sequence’s colour and name.
  • Sections — Fountain-style # section headings, nested by level, with their synopsis. When a script has both acts and sections, the Acts and Sections tabs in the header switch between the two views.
  • Scenes — each row shows the scene number, heading, synopsis (if you have written one) and length in pages, such as 1.5p.

A script with no acts or sections is shown as a plain list of scenes.

Reordering Scenes

Drag a scene row by its handle (⠿) and drop it on another row. Dragged down, the scene goes after the row you drop it on; dragged up, it goes before. The whole scene moves, and an act break just before its heading moves with it. Anything before the first scene heading, such as FADE IN:, stays where it is.

Each move is a single step for Edit > Undo.

Adding Acts, Sequences and Sections

The buttons in the Outline’s header add structure at the scene the cursor is in:

  • + Act — inserts an act break before the current scene (or at the end of a script with no scenes yet).
  • + Sequence — labels the current scene with a new sequence, named Sequence 1, Sequence 2… It needs at least one scene.
  • + Section — inserts a top-level section called New Section, ready to rename in the script.

Working With Sequences

A sequence is a label on scene headings, used to group the scenes of one stretch of the story (“the heist”, “the wedding”) without adding anything to the printed script.

  • Add a scene to a sequence — once the script has at least one sequence, every scene row has a Sequence list. Choose a sequence, or — to take the scene out of its sequence.
  • Rename — hover over a sequence’s header row and click the pencil. Type the new name and press Enter (or Esc to cancel). Every scene in the sequence takes the new name.
  • Delete — hover over the header row and click the bin. The label comes off all its scenes; the scenes themselves stay. Deleting can be undone.

The label is stored on each scene heading, so it moves with its scene, is shared with collaborators and goes along when you copy a scene into another script.

Good to know: Sections never print and don’t count towards page length. Final Draft and Fade In ignore OpenDraft’s sequence labels, so they are only visible again when the file is opened in OpenDraft.
