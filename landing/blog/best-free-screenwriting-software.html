The best free screenwriting software in 2026: an honest comparison

Plenty of screenwriting apps call themselves free. Some really are; others give you one project, watermark your PDFs, or stop working after a trial. We checked what each one’s free version actually allows today, using the vendors’ own pricing and download pages.

2 November 2026 · 8 minute read · facts checked 2 November 2026

Who wrote this

This guide is by the team behind OpenDraft, one of the apps below, so read it with that in mind. We’ve tried to be fair: every claim about another app links to that app’s own pricing or download page, as checked on 2 November 2026, and we say where another tool is the better choice. Prices and free plans change, so check before you commit.

The short answer

  • Free with no limits: OpenDraft, Beat (Mac), Trelby, and STARC’s free tier.
  • Free plans with limits: WriterDuet (3 projects), Arc Studio (2 scripts, watermarked PDFs), Celtx (1 active project) and StudioBinder (1 project).
  • Paid, with a free trial or demo: Final Draft, Fade In, Highland Pro and Scrite.

Lists of “free screenwriting software” age quickly, and a lot has changed recently. Highland 2’s free version has been replaced by a subscription app. Scrite, long recommended as free and open source, now needs a paid plan after a 30-day trial. KIT Scenarist has been archived. Here’s where things stand now.

Completely free

OpenDraft

OpenDraft is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no paid tier holding features back. It runs in a browser at open-draft.com and as an app for Mac, Windows, Linux, iPad, iPhone and Android, and it works offline.

  • Opens and saves Final Draft files; imports Fade In, Fountain, Highland, WriterDuet, Celtx and PDF scripts; exports PDF and Word.
  • Formats for film, one-hour TV, multi-camera sitcom, stage plays, radio and podcasts, and comic books.
  • Real-time collaboration through an invite link, a beat board, index cards, revision mode, version history, and production tagging.
  • A free Table Read that reads your script aloud with a voice for each character.

Good for: writers who want professional features without a subscription, on any device, and who care about keeping their scripts on their own machine.

Beat

Beat is a free, open-source (GPL-3.0) Mac app built around Fountain, the plain-text screenplay format, and it’s actively developed. It imports and exports Final Draft files. The iOS version is a small one-time purchase.

Good for: Mac users who like writing in plain text.

Trelby

Trelby is a long-standing open-source (GPL-2.0) desktop app for Windows and Linux. On a Mac it installs through Python’s package manager, which isn’t for everyone. It handles Final Draft files, but development has slowed: its most recent release was in October 2025.

Good for: Linux and Windows users who want a light, offline app.

STARC (Story Architect)

STARC, the successor to KIT Scenarist, is open source (GPL-3.0) with a generous free tier: unlimited projects and no watermark. Its planning tools (corkboard, timeline, mind maps), collaboration and sync are in the paid plans. It’s actively developed, with frequent releases.

Good for: writers who want a capable free desktop app and might pay later for planning tools.

Free plans with limits

WriterDuet

WriterDuet’s free plan allows three projects and works in the browser. Collaboration is its strength, but on the free plan collaborators can only read, and the desktop app is part of the paid plans.

Arc Studio

Arc Studio’s free plan gives you two scripts in the browser, with Fountain and Final Draft export. PDFs are watermarked, and you can join a collaboration but not invite people.

Celtx

Celtx moved online years ago. Its free plan allows one active project, with PDF export and no watermark. It no longer works offline: offline mode ended in September 2023. If you have old .celtx files from the discontinued desktop app, OpenDraft can open them.

StudioBinder

StudioBinder is mainly production software with a screenwriting tool attached. The free plan covers one project and one screenplay, and imports only half of an existing script.

Paid, with a free trial or demo

Final Draft

Final Draft is the long-standing industry standard and the format most productions exchange files in. It’s a paid app for Mac and Windows with a free trial. You don’t need it to read or write .fdx files: several apps here open and save them.

Fade In

Fade In is a professional app for Windows, Mac and Linux, sold as a one-time purchase with free updates. Its demo has nearly every feature, but it watermarks PDFs and printouts.

Highland Pro

Highland 2, and its free version, has been superseded by Highland Pro, a subscription app for Mac, iPad and iPhone with a 30-day trial. Older lists that call Highland free are out of date.

Scrite

Scrite is known for its support for Indian languages. Its source code is still open, but the official builds now need a paid plan after a 30-day trial, and trial output is watermarked. If you write in Hindi, Tamil or another Indian language, it’s worth trying.

Which one should you use?

  • Everything free, on any device: OpenDraft.
  • Plain-text writing on a Mac: Beat.
  • A free desktop app with optional paid planning tools: STARC.
  • Final Draft files without Final Draft: OpenDraft, Beat, Trelby or STARC. You can also convert an .fdx to PDF or Word in your browser.
  • A script someone sent you as a PDF: convert it into an editable script first.
  • Writing for theatre: see our guide to stage play format.

Questions

Is there a free version of Final Draft?

No. Final Draft offers a free trial, then it’s paid. Free apps such as OpenDraft, Beat, Trelby and STARC open and save Final Draft’s .fdx files, so you can work with Final Draft users without buying it.

Is Celtx still free?

It has a free plan, limited to one active project, that works only online. The old Celtx desktop app is discontinued.

What’s the best open-source screenwriting software?

OpenDraft (MIT), Beat (GPL-3.0), STARC (GPL-3.0) and Trelby (GPL-2.0) are all open source. OpenDraft runs on the most platforms, including the browser and phones. Beat is Mac and iOS only, and Trelby is the least actively developed.

Can I write a screenplay for free on an iPad or Android?

Yes. OpenDraft has free iPad, iPhone and Android apps. STARC has mobile apps, and Beat has an iOS app as a small one-time purchase.
